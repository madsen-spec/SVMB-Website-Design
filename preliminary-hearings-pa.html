Preliminary hearings in Pennsylvania

A preliminary hearing should be prepared from the complaint and the next date.

A preliminary hearing is often the first major event after charges are filed in a Pennsylvania criminal case. The immediate task is to identify the charge, court, hearing date, bail status and paperwork already available before deciding what to do next.

  • Criminal complaint and affidavit if available
  • Hearing notice and summons
  • Bail paperwork and conditions
  • Photos, messages, videos or witness names

What to do now

Do not treat a preliminary hearing as a routine appointment.

Preparation can affect bail, testimony, negotiation, waiver decisions and the path into Common Pleas.

Call promptly

When the hearing is scheduled

Call with the date, court, judge or magisterial district judge, charges and paperwork received.

Have ready

Complaint and bail papers

Bring the complaint, affidavit if available, bail conditions, summons, hearing notice and docket number.

Preserve records

Do not wait to save proof

Save photos, videos, texts, social media messages, location records and witness names if they may matter.

First call

Discuss the hearing posture

The first discussion should identify whether the hearing is contested, waived, continued or connected to negotiation.

Hearing preparation

The hearing date sets the pace for the first defense review.

A preliminary hearing is not a trial, but it can still affect how the case moves. The first defense review should look at the complaint, available witnesses, hearsay issues, bail conditions, discovery status, negotiation questions and whether waiving or contesting the hearing fits the facts.

The paperwork often arrives before the full evidence is available. Bring every page you have, including envelopes, the complaint, summons, bail conditions, police paperwork and any notice from probation or another agency.

Continuances are not automatic.

Do not assume a hearing will be moved just because more time would help. Call with the date and paperwork as soon as possible.

First-call details

  • Court and hearing date
  • Charge and grading
  • Bail status
  • Witness or evidence concerns

Records and witnesses

A few concrete records can matter more than a long story.

A useful review ties each fact to a document, witness, photo, video, message or timeline. If the record is incomplete, identify what exists and what is missing.

If police are asking for a statement before the hearing, call before speaking. A statement can affect charging, bail, negotiation and later testimony.

Complaint and affidavit

Bring the complaint and affidavit of probable cause if one was provided or is available.

Bail conditions

Identify reporting, contact, travel, alcohol, treatment, firearm, location or no-contact terms.

Witness information

Write down names, phone numbers and what each person may know, but do not pressure witnesses or alter records.

Electronic evidence

Preserve texts, photos, videos, social media posts, location records and phone data without editing originals.

Waiver questions

Do not waive a preliminary hearing without understanding what rights, strategy or later steps may be affected.

Related consequences

Raise work, school, licensing, immigration, custody, housing or firearms concerns during the first discussion.
