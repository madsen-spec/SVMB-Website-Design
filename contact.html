Contact

Contact the Easton office.

If a court date, hearing, arrest, license problem, PFA issue or agency date is close, call the office. For non-urgent questions, start with the basics: your name, how to reach you, the type of problem, the county, the court or agency if known, and the next date shown on any paper you received.

First call

The office will start with the basics

The first call or email usually identifies the type of problem, the timing, what papers matter most and whether a more detailed discussion can be scheduled.

How to make the first call easier

Keep the latest notice nearby.

The most useful details are usually printed on the notice that caused you to call: the county, court or agency, docket or claim number, next date and what the paper says must happen next.

For Northampton County matters, the paperwork may come from a magisterial district court, the Court of Common Pleas, Domestic Relations, PennDOT, the Pennsylvania Office of Unemployment Compensation, a zoning hearing board, a municipal office, or another agency. If you are not sure, read the heading on the paper.

If someone is in immediate danger, use emergency services first. For urgent legal papers, arrests, warrants, license issues, same-day court dates or agency deadlines, call the office directly.

For court deadlines

Call with the court, date and time, judge or magisterial district judge, docket number, and type of hearing. If papers were served, identify the service date and the date printed on the document. Continuances are not automatic.

For a new legal question

Use phone or email to ask whether the office can talk with you and what to bring. A short summary plus the most recent notice is usually more useful than a long message.

For names and parties

Have the names of other parties, employers, witnesses, businesses, insurance companies, or related people ready. Those names help the office sort the inquiry quickly.

For urgent court, agency, or license dates

If a paper lists a hearing, conference, appeal, answer, suspension, filing, or response date, call the office and identify that date. Email may not be the right first step for same-day or next-day issues.

What happens next

The first call or email can stay simple.

After the office has the basics, staff can usually tell you what to gather next and whether a fuller conversation can be scheduled.

  • If the issue is time-sensitive, staff may ask first for the court, agency, license, or deadline paper.
  • If a discussion can be scheduled, the office can tell you what to bring or send next.
  • If the firm cannot help with the issue, the office may be able to tell you before you spend more time gathering records.
  • If more information is needed, the next step will usually be discussed by phone or in person.

The goal is simple: identify the problem, where it is pending, the next date and the paper or event that brought you to the office.
