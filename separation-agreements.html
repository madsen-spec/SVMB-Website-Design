Separation agreements

A separation agreement should be reviewed before it shapes your finances or family plan.

Separation agreements can affect property, debt, support, household expenses, insurance, custody-related arrangements and later divorce planning. Do not treat a proposed agreement as routine just because it looks informal.

Agreements and divorce planning

The wording can affect more than the immediate separation.

A separation agreement may address property, debt, support, household expenses, residence, insurance, bank accounts, taxes, retirement accounts and other issues that can later affect divorce planning.

Some terms are easy to misunderstand without reviewing the underlying records. A payment term, account transfer, debt responsibility, waiver, custody-related schedule or insurance obligation may have practical consequences beyond the sentence where it appears.

SVMB Law helps Lehigh Valley clients review proposed separation terms, organize the financial record and decide what issues should be clarified before an agreement is signed or relied upon.

Do not rely on a summary.

Bring the full proposed agreement and the records tied to each major term. Short summaries can omit important conditions or deadlines.

Useful first-call details

  • Property and debt
  • Income and support
  • Insurance and taxes
  • Custody-related terms

Before signing

A focused review can identify what is missing or unclear.

Bring account statements, deeds, titles, loan records, tax returns, retirement records, insurance information and any messages discussing the proposed terms.

If custody, support, PFA or divorce papers already exist, bring those too. Agreement terms should be read alongside current orders and any pending court dates.

Property terms

Identify real estate, vehicles, accounts, retirement assets, business interests and personal property covered by the agreement.

Debt terms

List mortgages, loans, credit cards, tax obligations and other debts, including who pays and whether refinancing or transfer is required.

Support and expenses

Gather income records, household bills, insurance costs, childcare costs and any support order or proposed payment terms.

Custody-related language

Bring existing custody orders and proposed schedule language if the agreement mentions parenting time, transportation or child expenses.

Missing records

Make a list of account statements, valuations, payoff records or tax documents that are not yet available.

Before contacting the office

Have the proposed agreement, deadline, divorce status, current orders and involved party names ready for conflict-check purposes.
