Crash claim preparation

A claim is easier to review when the record is organized from the start.

After a crash or injury, the first legal questions are practical: what happened, who was involved, what insurance applies, what treatment has occurred and what proof may disappear. Save photos, reports, medical papers, bills, wage information and insurance messages early.

Injury claims can have filing deadlines and notice requirements that depend on the facts, parties and claim type. The actual deadline should be checked early, especially if a public entity, minor, delayed diagnosis, workers' compensation overlap or unusual notice issue may be involved.

Car crash questions may also involve full-tort or limited-tort policy language, uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, commercial vehicles, motorcycles, trucks, rideshare vehicles or work-related driving. Bring policy papers and any letters from all insurers involved.

SVMB Law helps Lehigh Valley clients organize crash records, identify missing proof, review insurance communications and discuss practical next steps without promising a settlement, value or result.

Do not sign releases without review.

A release, medical authorization or settlement check can affect rights and records. Preserve the full document and ask questions before signing.

Evidence and treatment

The crash record and medical record need to line up.

A useful claim review connects the crash proof to the medical timeline. Keep emergency-room papers, urgent-care records, referrals, therapy records, restrictions, prescriptions and bills.

For crashes investigated by Pennsylvania State Police, report requests may use the PSP crash-report process. For local departments, preserve the incident number and officer or department information.

Crash report

Bring the report number, police department, officer name if known, crash date, location and any citation information.

Scene evidence

Save photos, video, dash camera footage, vehicle damage photos, road-condition photos and witness names.

Medical timeline

List providers, visits, referrals, restrictions, prescriptions, therapy and symptoms by date.

Insurance communications

Save claim numbers, adjuster letters, emails, recorded statement requests, authorizations, release forms, policy declarations and any full-tort, limited-tort, uninsured or underinsured motorist paperwork.

Work and expenses

Track missed work, wage records, mileage, prescriptions, copays, repair estimates and out-of-pocket costs.

Before emailing

Use email only for basic contact information until the office confirms a conflict check and tells you what to send.
