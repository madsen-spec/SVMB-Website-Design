Slip, fall and unsafe property claims

Fall injury claims depend on the condition, the timing and the proof.

A fall or unsafe property injury may involve a spill, ice, broken surface, poor lighting, stairs, maintenance issue or other condition. The first call should focus on what happened, where it happened, who controlled the property and what proof still exists.

Unsafe property preparation

Photos and reports can matter because the property may change quickly.

Slip, trip and fall claims can involve stores, sidewalks, parking lots, homes, apartments, offices, public spaces or other properties. The early questions are practical: what condition caused the fall, how long it may have existed, who controlled the area and what records show it.

The record may include photos, incident reports, witness names, maintenance records, weather information, manager communications, medical treatment, bills and wage-loss proof. The website cannot decide whether a property owner is responsible for a specific fall.

Take photos before repairs or cleanup.

If it is safe, preserve photos of the exact condition, surrounding area, shoes, lighting, warnings and weather or surface details.

Property and treatment records

The condition and the treatment timeline both matter.

A useful review connects the fall condition to the treatment record. Save emergency papers, provider lists, referrals, restrictions, bills and photos of visible injuries if appropriate.

If an insurer, manager or property owner asks for a statement or release, preserve the request before responding.

Photos and video

Preserve photos of the condition, surrounding area, warnings, lighting, footwear and injuries. Identify video sources if any.

Incident report

Ask for a copy if one exists. Write down who took the report, when it was made and what was said.

Property details

Identify the owner, tenant, store, property manager, municipality or other entity that may control the area.

Witness information

Save names, phone numbers and what each person saw. Do not rely on memory if contact details are available.

Medical records

Keep discharge papers, provider lists, referrals, restrictions, prescriptions and follow-up records.

Insurance contact

Save letters, claim numbers, adjuster names, recorded statement requests, authorizations and release forms.
