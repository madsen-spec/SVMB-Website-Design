Local workplace issues

Start with the notice, timing and workplace record.

Employment problems often start with a dated paper: a termination letter, unemployment determination, discipline notice, wage record, agency notice or workplace message. Save the notice, envelope, portal message and any instructions that came with it.

For unemployment claims, the reason for separation matters. A discharge, voluntary quit, refusal of work, layoff, job abandonment or alleged misconduct can point to different proof, witnesses and hearing issues.

UC, PHRC, EEOC and court deadlines should be checked against the actual paper, facts and agency involved. Do not assume an internal HR process, employer investigation, union process or informal discussion pauses an outside deadline.

Employment services

Common employment issues that need organized proof.

Employment and unemployment issues often depend on written notices, dated communications and testimony about what happened at work. The earlier those records are organized, the easier it is to see what should happen next.

Unemployment appeals and referee hearings

Claimants and employers may need to address discharge, voluntary quit, job abandonment, refusal of work, availability, willful misconduct, witness testimony, exhibits and whether the appeal was filed on time. The determination, appeal confirmation and hearing notice usually frame the dispute.

Termination and discipline disputes

Discipline, performance evaluations, attendance records, policy acknowledgments, HR communications and witness information can affect how a workplace dispute is evaluated. Preserve original documents and messages when possible.

Wage, hour and compensation concerns

Pay stubs, schedules, time records, commission records, deductions, classification details and benefit records help identify what questions need legal review. Do not rely only on memory for hours worked or wages owed.

Discrimination and retaliation timing

Agency filing windows may be short. Preserve documents showing what happened, who was involved, when it happened, when it was reported and when an adverse action occurred. Internal HR processes do not always pause outside agency deadlines.

Unemployment hearing preparation

A referee hearing is evidence-driven.

A referee hearing is evidence-driven. The hearing notice usually identifies the issues and gives instructions for attendance, exhibits and witnesses. In many cases, the main work is building a clear timeline, identifying which facts are disputed and matching records or testimony to the issues listed on the notice.

For a claimant, the record may include the separation reason, communications with the employer, job-search or availability information and any UC system messages. For an employer, it may include policies, attendance records, warnings, witness testimony and the stated reason for separation.

The hearing materials should be organized by date. A short timeline should include hire date, separation date, final incident or reason for leaving, UC filing date, determination date, appeal filing date and hearing date.

Employment FAQ

Common questions before you call.

What should I do after receiving an unemployment determination?

Save the full determination, envelope, online messages and any appeal instructions. If you disagree with the decision, call promptly because Pennsylvania unemployment appeal deadlines are short and the notice date matters.

What documents help in an unemployment referee hearing?

Bring the hearing notice, determination, appeal confirmation, employer communications, policies, warnings, pay records, witness names and any exhibits that address the issues listed on the notice.

What if the employer says I was fired for misconduct?

Bring the policy, warning, attendance record, schedule, messages, incident report, witness names and any records showing what happened. A misconduct dispute usually turns on specific facts, not a general statement that the separation was unfair.

Can I wait to see whether a workplace issue gets better?

Waiting can make some employment claims harder to evaluate because documents disappear, witnesses move on and agency filing windows may pass. Preserve records and ask for advice before a deadline runs.

Does every unfair workplace decision create a legal claim?

No. Employment law depends on the facts, records, statutes, employer conduct and where the issue can be raised. An attorney can help sort whether the issue is legal, internal, administrative or civil.
