Plan a civil matter

After an arrest or criminal complaint

Bring the complaint, affidavit if available, bail papers, hearing notices and any messages or video that may affect the facts.

  • Preliminary hearings
  • Bail, detainers and warrants
  • Negotiation, trial preparation and sentencing issues

After a DUI or traffic citation

License consequences can move separately from the criminal case. Save the citation, chemical-test paperwork and PennDOT notices.

  • ARD and negotiated resolutions
  • License suspensions and appeals
  • Summary appeals and points issues

Before a custody or support conference

Gather existing orders, schedules, school information, income records, expenses and communications about parenting issues.

  • Custody complaints and modifications
  • Child support and spousal support
  • PFA filings and contempt concerns

When a civil dispute is growing

Preserve contracts, photos, letters, emails, estimates, medical records and timelines before evidence becomes harder to organize.

  • Personal injury claims
  • Employment and unemployment matters
  • Land use, landlord-tenant and business disputes

Client path

What to expect when you contact the firm.

The first conversation is used to understand the type of case, the court or agency involved and whether there are immediate deadlines.

  1. 1

    Share the basic facts

    Start with the county, court date, opposing party, charges or claims, and the result you need help protecting.

  2. 2

    Bring the documents that control the timeline

    Orders, complaints, citations, notices, contracts and letters usually matter more than a long verbal history at the start.

  3. 3

    Review options and next steps

    An attorney can help evaluate risk, set priorities, explain likely procedure and prepare for the next filing, hearing or negotiation.

  4. 4

    Stay organized as the matter moves

    Keep new notices, emails, texts, photos and records together so the legal strategy can adjust as facts develop.

Frequently asked questions

Questions to answer before the first call.

I have a court date soon. What should I do first?

Call the office and identify the county, court, date, type of case and any paperwork you received. Do not ignore a hearing notice or assume the date will be continued.

Can I send confidential facts through the website?

No. Use the form only for basic contact information and a short description. Do not send privileged, emergency or highly sensitive information until an attorney-client relationship is confirmed.

What documents should I bring to a consultation?

Bring court papers, citations, orders, notices, contracts, photos, messages, medical records, income information and any deadline-related letters.

Does contacting the firm create an attorney-client relationship?

No. An attorney-client relationship is created only after the firm confirms it can represent you and the required engagement terms are completed.

Can the firm guarantee a result?

No lawyer can guarantee a result. The firm can evaluate the facts, explain risks and help you prepare for the next legal step.
