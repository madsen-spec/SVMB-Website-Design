Personal injury

Careful case review after an injury disrupts work, health or daily life.

A personal injury claim depends on liability, causation, insurance coverage, medical treatment and proof of damages. Early organization helps preserve the record.

Useful documents

  • Photos, incident reports and witness names
  • Medical records and discharge papers
  • Insurance letters and claim numbers
  • Wage loss, mileage and out-of-pocket costs

Injury claims

Evidence can become harder to collect over time.

After an accident or injury, it is important to preserve photographs, reports, medical records, repair estimates, insurance communications and witness information.

The firm can review the facts, identify potential claims, evaluate insurance issues and help document damages without promising a particular result.

Claims may involve

  • Vehicle crashes and pedestrian injuries
  • Falls and unsafe property conditions
  • Insurance disputes and coverage questions
  • Medical bills, lost wages and future impact

Before giving a recorded statement

Insurance communications may affect the claim. If you are unsure what to provide, seek legal guidance before giving a recorded statement.

Track losses

  • Medical appointments
  • Missed work
  • Travel and mileage
  • Prescription and equipment costs

Liability review

Liability may depend on documents, scene evidence, witnesses, policy language and comparative fault. The earlier those materials are preserved, the easier the review becomes.

Damages documentation

Medical records, treatment plans, wage records and daily-life impact help explain the scope of harm. Keep copies of bills and correspondence.
