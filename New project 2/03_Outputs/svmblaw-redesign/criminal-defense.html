Criminal defense

Defense guidance when charges, warrants or investigations threaten your future.

Criminal cases move through deadlines, hearings and decisions that can affect liberty, employment, licensing, family obligations and immigration concerns.

Call promptly if you have

  • An arrest, summons or criminal complaint
  • A preliminary hearing or arraignment date
  • A warrant, detainer or probation issue
  • Police contact about an investigation

Defense work

Early decisions can shape the case.

Before speaking with police, waiving a hearing, entering a plea or missing a court date, it is important to understand what the Commonwealth must prove and what options may be available.

The firm can help evaluate charging documents, identify key facts, prepare for preliminary hearings, discuss negotiation options and prepare for trial or sentencing where needed.

Common criminal matters

  • Misdemeanor and felony charges
  • Theft, assault, harassment and terroristic threats allegations
  • Drug offenses, firearms offenses and probation violations
  • Juvenile cases and PFA contempt matters
  • Record-related issues and collateral consequences

Do not ignore court papers.

Missing a criminal court date can create additional problems. If you are unsure what a document means, call the office and identify the county and hearing date.

Bring these documents

  • Criminal complaint and affidavit if available
  • Bail paperwork and notices
  • Citations, summonses or warrants
  • Photos, messages, video or witness names

Preliminary hearings

The preliminary hearing is often the first major court event in a Pennsylvania criminal case. Preparation may involve reviewing the complaint, identifying witness issues and deciding whether to contest, waive or negotiate.

Negotiation and trial preparation

Resolution may involve diversion, reduced charges, plea negotiations, trial or sentencing advocacy. The right path depends on the record, evidence, client goals and practical risk.
