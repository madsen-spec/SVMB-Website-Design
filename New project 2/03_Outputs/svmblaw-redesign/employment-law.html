Employment law

Workplace disputes often turn on timing, records and agency deadlines.

Employment issues may involve unemployment compensation, wage concerns, discipline, termination, discrimination claims or civil disputes. The record should be organized early.

Gather before calling

  • Termination, discipline or HR letters
  • Unemployment notices and hearing dates
  • Pay records, schedules and handbook pages
  • Relevant emails, texts and witness names

Workplace matters

Agency deadlines and written records matter.

Employment disputes can move through internal HR processes, unemployment compensation proceedings, administrative agencies or civil litigation. Each forum has its own deadlines and proof requirements.

The firm can help evaluate paperwork, prepare for unemployment hearings, review workplace communications and identify practical next steps.

Common issues

  • Unemployment compensation hearings and appeals
  • Termination, discipline and workplace disputes
  • Wage, hour and compensation concerns
  • Administrative claims and civil employment disputes

Do not wait on hearing notices.

Unemployment hearings and agency deadlines often arrive quickly. Save envelopes, notices and online messages showing dates.

Helpful details

  • Employer name and work location
  • Dates of hire, discipline and termination
  • Names of managers or witnesses
  • Benefits or agency deadlines

Unemployment hearings

Preparation includes understanding the stated reason for separation, gathering exhibits and identifying testimony that addresses the agency's issues.

Workplace disputes

Emails, handbooks, pay records, disciplinary records and witness information help evaluate the dispute and decide whether negotiation or formal action makes sense.
