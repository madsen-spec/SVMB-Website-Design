Changing an order

The current order is the starting point, not background paperwork.

Many custody problems start because life has changed after an order was entered. Work schedules, school needs, transportation, distance, communication, safety concerns and parenting availability can all affect what a parent asks the court to change.

A modification request should not be built from frustration alone. The useful questions are what the order says, how the schedule is working now, what changed, what child-related facts support the request and what schedule is realistic.

SVMB Law helps Lehigh Valley clients organize modification papers, prepare for conferences or hearings and focus the request around the records that matter for the next court step.

Do not rely on memory alone.

Bring the order and records that show dates. Text messages, school notices and calendars can help only if they are organized and complete.

Useful first-call details

  • Order language
  • Actual parenting calendar
  • Dated changes
  • Proposed new schedule

Conference preparation

A clear proposal helps the court understand what is being requested.

Before a custody conference or hearing, prepare a specific schedule proposal. Include weekdays, weekends, holidays, exchanges, transportation, school breaks and communication terms.

If the other parent has already filed, bring every page served on you, including notices and envelopes. If you are considering filing, bring the current order and the facts that explain why a change may be needed.

What the order says

Read the exact custody terms before calling. Identify legal custody, physical custody, holiday terms, transportation and communication language.

What is happening now

Write down the schedule being followed, missed exchanges, informal changes, school issues and communication patterns.

What changed

Organize dates tied to work schedules, school needs, relocation, safety issues, health concerns, transportation limits or other child-related facts.

What you are asking for

Prepare a realistic requested schedule with days, times, exchange places and communication terms.

PFA, support or divorce overlap

Tell the office if another family-court case, PFA order, support case or divorce filing affects the custody issue.

First-call information

Have the county, docket number, current order, next date, involved party names and conflict-safe contact information ready.
