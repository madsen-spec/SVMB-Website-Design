Insurance adjuster questions

Before responding to an adjuster, know what is being requested.

After an injury, an insurer may ask for a statement, medical authorization, release, wage information, photos or other records. Save the request and identify what it asks for before deciding how to respond.

Insurance communication records

The request matters as much as the claim number.

Before responding to an adjuster, identify exactly what is being requested. A call for basic claim information is different from a recorded statement, medical authorization, release, settlement paper or broad request for records.

Save the request, claim number, adjuster contact information and any deadline listed. Do not assume a document is routine just because it came from an insurer.

Save the full request.

Keep the letter, form, email or text. If the request came by phone, write down the date, time, caller, company and claim number.

First-call details

  • Insurer and claim number
  • Adjuster name and contact
  • What was requested
  • Treatment and work status

Claim documents

Insurance records should be organized before major decisions.

Separate the claim-opening documents, medical-record requests, settlement papers, repair estimates, wage-loss requests and correspondence. Keep envelopes and email dates when possible.

If a release or settlement check has arrived, preserve the full packet and do not sign until the language and effect are understood.

Recorded statements

Save the request and ask what topic, insurer and claim it concerns before deciding how to respond.

Medical authorizations

Broad authorizations can affect private records. Preserve the form and related letter before signing.

Releases

A release or settlement document may affect future rights. Bring the entire packet, not only the signature page.

Claim numbers

Record the insurer, adjuster, claim number, policyholder and contact information for each insurance company involved.

Treatment status

Identify whether treatment is ongoing, referrals are pending, bills are incomplete or work restrictions continue.

Communication log

Keep a simple dated list of calls, emails, letters, texts and documents sent or received.
