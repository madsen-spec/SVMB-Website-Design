The exact wording is important.

Firearms questions should start with the exact paper involved: the charge, order, denial, notice, license-to-carry letter, PICS paper, relinquishment term, probation condition or agency correspondence.

Do not rely on a general online description or memory of an old case to decide what you may possess, carry, buy, transfer, challenge, store or seek returned. Firearms consequences can depend on Pennsylvania law, federal law, prior records, court orders, agency action and the precise wording of the paper in front of you. If a notice lists a response date, preserve it and call promptly.

Papers to gather

  • criminal complaints, charging papers, sentencing orders, probation papers, docket sheets, and disposition records;
  • PFA orders, temporary or final orders, service papers, and any firearm, weapon, ammunition, safekeeping, return, or relinquishment terms;
  • license-to-carry application, renewal, revocation, suspension, denial, sheriff, or county paperwork;
  • PICS denial, undetermined-status, challenge, dealer, Pennsylvania State Police, or other agency correspondence;
  • prior Pennsylvania, federal, military, juvenile, mental-health, expungement, pardon, restoration, or out-of-state records if they may be relevant;
  • all envelopes, dates, case numbers, agency numbers and response dates shown on notices.

Do not guess about possession.

If a court order, PFA term, criminal charge, license notice, or agency paper may affect firearm possession, carrying, purchase, transfer, storage or return, get advice on the exact document before taking action.

Overlapping records

Criminal, PFA and licensing records should be looked at together.

A firearms-related question should start with the actual court order, charge, notice, agency letter, denial letter, licensing paper or PFA term. Bring every page and identify all dates, case numbers, agencies, counties, prior orders and related court dates.

The firm cannot evaluate eligibility, risk or possible next steps from a general description alone. The exact papers and record history are important, especially when a firearms question overlaps with a criminal case, family-court order, protection order, probation condition, sheriff decision or state agency record.

Criminal-case overlap

Bring the complaint, grading, docket sheet, bail order, sentencing order, probation paperwork, diversion or ARD records, and final disposition for any case that may affect the firearms question. Older cases can matter, so include old docket numbers, expungement papers, pardon records or disposition proof if available.

PFA or domestic-case overlap

Bring every temporary or final PFA order, petition, service paper, contempt or violation notice, custody order and related criminal paperwork. If an order mentions firearms, weapons, ammunition, residence, safekeeping, return or relinquishment, the exact wording and service date matter.

License-to-carry issues

Bring the application, renewal, denial, revocation, suspension, sheriff correspondence, county paperwork and any date listed on the notice. Identify the county, date of decision, reason given if any, and any related criminal, PFA, mental-health or record-history issue mentioned in the paperwork.

PICS or purchase-transfer questions

If the issue involves a purchase, transfer, denial, undetermined result, challenge or agency letter, bring the dealer paperwork, PICS-related notice, Pennsylvania State Police correspondence and any records that may explain the background-check issue. Short timing rules can apply, so do not wait to organize the documents.

Record history and restoration questions

Eligibility and restoration questions may require old court files, certified dispositions, agency records, mental-health records, expungement orders, pardon materials or out-of-state papers. The first discussion should identify what records exist, what is missing and whether more documentation is needed before advice can be given.

Before the first call

Have the county, agency, docket number, license or PICS reference, exact notice language, related court dates and prior-record information ready. Keep confidential details out of email until the office confirms it can discuss the issue with you.
