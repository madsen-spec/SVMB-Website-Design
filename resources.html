Consultation preparation

Start with what brought you here.

Most calls are easier when the latest notice or letter is nearby: a court notice, criminal complaint, custody order, PFA hearing notice, PennDOT letter, unemployment determination, agency notice, insurance request, contract demand or lawsuit paper.

Add a short timeline with names, dates and copies of the key records. For family, criminal, employment, injury and civil issues, a few organized facts are more useful than a long unsorted history.

If the paper says you must appear, answer, appeal, respond or file by a date, call the office with that paper in front of you.

Working packet

A good packet can be simple.

You do not need a perfect file before calling. A small, clear packet is often enough to begin.

  • Put the newest dated notice or court paper at the front.
  • Sort records by date.
  • Keep envelopes and cover letters when they show mailing or receipt dates.
  • Label photos with the date, location, and what they show.
  • Save text messages, emails, portal messages, and letters with visible dates.
  • Separate court filings, agency notices, medical records, financial records, and correspondence.
  • Make a one-page timeline before adding long background notes.

Do not alter, delete, redact, annotate, or mark up original records. Keep originals separate and use copies for discussion.

Dates can come from different places

A date may depend on the court, agency, claim type, service date, mailing date, event date or words in the notice. If more than one date appears, write each one down separately.

Keep the actual notice, envelope, email, dashboard message or court paper. General online summaries cannot confirm what your specific paper requires.

  • County, court, agency, claim number and case number if known
  • Next hearing, response date or other listed date
  • Names of involved parties and witnesses
  • Copies of notices, orders, letters, messages and agency communications

Criminal or DUI matters

Bring citations, complaints, bail papers, summonses, warrants, hearing notices, PennDOT letters, chemical-test paperwork, refusal paperwork, ARD papers, and any messages, photos, or videos. Identify the county, arresting agency, court, next date, and license-related notices.

Family law and custody issues

Bring current orders, petitions, conference notices, parenting schedules, school records, medical records, childcare records, income documents, expense records, messages, and proposed schedule ideas. Identify any PFA, relocation, contempt, support, custody, or divorce-related deadline.

Employment and unemployment issues

Bring termination letters, disciplinary records, handbooks, schedules, pay records, HR messages, unemployment determinations, hearing notices, agency paperwork, witness names, and a dated timeline. Identify the employer, work location, separation date, agency, and next deadline.

Personal injury issues

Bring photographs, reports, medical records, bills, insurance letters, claim numbers, wage-loss records, repair estimates, and any request for a statement, authorization, release, or recorded interview.

Land use and civil issues

Bring contracts, leases, deeds, notices, permits, photographs, maps, invoices, estimates, correspondence, board papers, municipal notices, and any complaint, summons, hearing notice, demand letter, or appeal instruction.
