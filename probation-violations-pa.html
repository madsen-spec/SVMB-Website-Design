Probation violation questions

Probation violation concerns start with the order and the alleged violation.

A probation issue may involve a new charge, missed appointment, unpaid obligation, failed test, treatment issue, technical allegation, detainer or violation hearing. The first call should identify the supervision terms, alleged violation, county and next date.

Violation preparation

The paperwork should show what the court or probation office says happened.

Probation violation questions are fact-specific. The first review should identify the original sentence, probation terms, alleged violation, whether a detainer exists, whether new charges are involved and what hearing or conference is scheduled next.

A technical allegation may require different records than a new-offense allegation. Payment records, treatment attendance, employment records, medical information, testing documents, travel permissions and probation communications may all matter depending on the claim.

SVMB Law helps clients in the Lehigh Valley organize probation and criminal-court records, prepare for violation proceedings and identify what information should be gathered before the next court event.

A detainer is time-sensitive.

If someone is detained or a warrant has been issued, call with the court, docket number, probation office and any available paperwork.

First-call details

  • Original sentence and judge
  • Probation officer or office
  • Violation allegation
  • Detainer or hearing status

Records that may matter

Violation preparation should be tied to documents, not assumptions.

Bring paperwork showing the condition at issue and any record that supports compliance, context or a changed circumstance. Examples may include treatment attendance, payment history, medical records, employment records or messages from probation.

If the violation is based on a new arrest or charge, bring the new complaint, bail paperwork, preliminary hearing notice and any police or court documents received.

Original order

Bring the sentencing order, probation conditions, payment terms and any special restrictions.

Violation notice

Bring the violation petition, detainer notice, hearing date or letter from probation or the court.

Technical allegation

Gather reporting, treatment, payment, testing, travel, employment or communication records tied to the alleged condition.

New charge

If a new offense is alleged, bring the complaint, bail papers, hearing notice and police paperwork if available.

Detainer concerns

Identify where the person is held, who issued the detainer, whether bail exists and what hearing is scheduled.

Collateral issues

Raise work, family, treatment, housing, license or immigration concerns during the first discussion.
