Evidence and timing should be checked early.

After an accident or injury, legal evaluation starts with what happened, where it happened, who may be responsible, what insurance applies and what medical harm can be documented. The facts are usually clearer when photos, reports, medical records and insurance communications are preserved from the beginning.

Personal injury claims can have filing deadlines and notice requirements that depend on the facts, parties and claim type. The actual deadline should be checked early against the records, especially when a public entity, minor, delayed diagnosis, workers' compensation overlap or unusual notice issue may be involved.

For vehicle crashes, reports may come from the investigating police department or, for Pennsylvania State Police investigations, through the PSP crash report process. Copies are not always available immediately, so it is useful to save the incident number, date, location and officer or department information.

For falls or property-related injuries, the record may include photos of the condition, incident reports, weather conditions, maintenance records, property communications, witness information and medical treatment.

Injury claim issues

What helps after an accident.

Personal injury cases are fact-specific. At the beginning, the goal is to understand the incident, preserve evidence and identify what information is still missing.

Vehicle crash claims

Crash location, police reports, photos, driver information, insurance information, medical treatment, repair estimates and witness names help evaluate responsibility and coverage. If an insurer calls quickly, save the claim number and adjuster information before giving detailed statements.

Falls and unsafe property conditions

Photos, incident reports, maintenance records, notice of the condition and the timing of cleanup or repair can be important in premises matters. Evidence may disappear quickly if a spill, ice condition, broken step or dangerous surface is repaired or cleaned.

Insurance and release questions

Adjuster letters, policy information, recorded statement requests, medical authorizations and release forms should be evaluated before major claim decisions are made. A release or broad authorization can affect rights and privacy.

Medical and wage loss documentation

Treatment records, bills, referrals, restrictions, wage records and daily impact notes help explain how the injury affected health, work and normal life. A provider list and treatment timeline are often more useful than a general statement that the injury was serious.

Before the first discussion

The first review should connect what happened to the harm suffered.

A useful injury evaluation ties together the incident, the evidence of fault, the insurance available and the medical record. A clear timeline can show when symptoms began, where treatment occurred, what restrictions were given and whether the injury affected work or normal routines.

Bring all correspondence from insurers, copies of medical paperwork, pharmacy records, photos, repair estimates, wage records and any letters asking for a statement or signature. Do not rely on memory alone if records are available.

If medical treatment is still ongoing, the consultation can still be useful. Early legal evaluation may identify missing reports, evidence that should be preserved, insurance questions, lien issues, wage-loss documentation or deadline concerns.

Personal injury FAQ

Common questions before you call.

What should I do first after an accident?

Get needed medical care, preserve photos and reports, save insurance communications and write down names of witnesses or agencies involved. Call promptly if a deadline, release, recorded statement request or lawsuit paper arrives.

Should I speak with the insurance adjuster?

Basic claim setup may be necessary, but recorded statements, broad authorizations and releases can affect the claim. Ask what is being requested and preserve the communication before deciding how to respond.

How long do I have to bring an injury claim?

Injury claims can have filing deadlines and notice requirements that depend on the facts, parties and claim type. The actual deadline should be checked early against the records, especially when a public entity, minor, delayed diagnosis, workers' compensation overlap or unusual notice issue may be involved.

What makes a consultation more useful?

Bring a short timeline, photos, incident reports, provider names, medical paperwork, bills, insurance letters, claim numbers and wage-loss information. If something is missing, bring what you have and identify what still needs to be obtained.
