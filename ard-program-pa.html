ARD questions in Pennsylvania

ARD questions start with the charge, the date and the record.

Accelerated Rehabilitative Disposition, often called ARD, may come up after a DUI or other criminal charge. Do not assume eligibility from a general online description or another person's case. The discussion should start with the charge, prior history, county process, victim or restitution issue, prosecutor review and any PennDOT paperwork.

ARD preparation

ARD is a process to evaluate, not a result to promise.

For many people, the first ARD question is simple: am I eligible. The safer starting point is broader. Identify the charge, county, next court date, prior record, testing paperwork, any accident or victim issue, restitution question, treatment paperwork and PennDOT mail.

In DUI cases, Pennsylvania's DUI statutes and local court procedures may affect what paperwork appears, what conditions are discussed and what license questions need attention. Court paperwork and PennDOT notices should be read together because they may not move on the same schedule.

SVMB Law can help clients in the Lehigh Valley organize the criminal complaint, evaluate what questions need attorney review, prepare for court events and understand what documents may still be missing before any ARD decision is discussed.

Do not miss the next court date.

ARD questions do not pause a preliminary hearing, arraignment, PennDOT notice or other date unless the court or agency paperwork says so.

Court and license records

Court papers and PennDOT papers may point to different issues.

A DUI case can create court obligations, treatment paperwork, testing records and separate license notices. An ARD discussion should not treat the license record as an afterthought.

Bring every page, including envelopes. The date on a notice may matter, and the complete packet often shows what is missing.

Eligibility questions

Be ready to discuss the charge, prior record, prior ARD, facts of the incident, accident or victim issues and county process.

Court papers

Bring the complaint, citation, preliminary hearing notice, arraignment papers, bail paperwork and any ARD packet or instruction.

License papers

Bring PennDOT notices, restoration letters, suspension notices, testing or refusal paperwork and current license status information.

Treatment or assessment records

If an assessment, class or treatment paper has been issued, bring the full instruction and any proof of completion.

What not to assume

Do not assume acceptance, dismissal, expungement, license outcome or timing from another person's case.
