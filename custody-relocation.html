Relocation questions need quick attention and a workable parenting plan.

A proposed move can affect school, transportation, holidays, exchanges and each parent's time with a child. If relocation papers were served or received, the first call should focus on the notice, objection timing, current order and proposed schedule.

Relocation and custody

A move can change more than an address.

Pennsylvania custody relocation issues can involve notice, objections, hearing preparation and a revised parenting schedule. A parent should not assume that an informal agreement or preferred calendar is enough when court papers or a current custody order exist.

Relocation disputes often turn on daily logistics: school placement, distance, transportation, work schedules, childcare, medical care, extended family support and how the child will communicate with each parent.

SVMB Law helps parents in the Lehigh Valley organize relocation notices, current custody orders, proposed schedules and supporting records before the next filing, response or hearing.

Response timing matters.

Relocation papers may include a counter-affidavit and a short objection period. Use the exact paper and service date as the starting point.

Useful first-call details

  • Service date

  • Current custody order
  • Proposed schedule

Planning the schedule

The proposed schedule should answer real-life questions.

A relocation plan should address exchanges, transportation costs, school breaks, holidays, virtual communication, medical appointments, extracurricular activities and the child's connection to each community.

If opposing relocation, organize specific concerns about the move, distance, school, transportation, communication, safety, work schedules or loss of time. The court will need facts, not only objections.

Notice and service

Bring the relocation notice, proof of service, counter-affidavit and any hearing notice or court order.

Current custody terms

Identify the existing custody schedule, holiday terms, transportation language and communication rules.

Move details

Gather the proposed address, school information, work schedule, housing information and expected move date.

Transportation plan

Prepare realistic exchange locations, driving times, travel responsibilities and school-break options.

Child-related facts

Organize facts about school, healthcare, activities, family support, friendships and the child's daily routine.

Before contacting the office

Have the county, docket number, service date, proposed move date, current order and party names ready for conflict-check purposes.
