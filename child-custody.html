Custody preparation

Specific child-related facts are stronger than general frustration.

Custody issues can involve legal custody, physical custody, holidays, transportation, school decisions, medical decisions, communication and exchanges. A useful first discussion separates the adult conflict from the facts that affect the child's routine and safety.

SVMB Law works with parents and family members in Easton, Bethlehem, Allentown, Northampton County, Lehigh County and the surrounding Lehigh Valley on custody complaints, conferences, hearings, modifications and related family-court issues.

The firm takes a practical, focused approach: identify the current order, identify the next court event, organize the records and decide what must be addressed now instead of doing unnecessary work before the basic posture is clear.

Keep examples dated.

A few dated examples tied to school, health, safety, transportation, communication or exchanges usually help more than a long unsorted history.

Useful first-call details

  • What order exists now
  • What schedule is actually being followed
  • What schedule is requested
  • What date, notice or filing is next

Local custody context

County procedure and the child's routine both matter.

A custody case in Northampton County or Lehigh County may involve conferences, service requirements, proposed orders, hearing notices and local scheduling rules. The paperwork often tells you where the case is and what date matters next.

Bring the child's school schedule, medical information, childcare details, transportation limits and any records showing communication problems or schedule disruptions. The more concrete the facts, the easier it is to focus the next step.

Current order

Bring the complete current order, any later modifications and any written agreement the parties have been following.

Actual schedule

Write down the schedule the child has actually followed, including weekdays, weekends, holidays, exchange locations and transportation.

Proposed schedule

A specific proposal with days, times, exchange points and communication terms is easier to discuss than a general request.

School and medical records

Bring school attendance, report cards, teacher messages, medical records, counseling records and childcare information when those facts are part of the dispute.

Conference or GAL papers

Bring conference notices, scheduling orders, proposed parenting plans, background forms, guardian ad litem papers, child-attorney papers and any court instructions about what must be completed before the next event.

Safety or PFA overlap

Identify any PFA order, police involvement, supervised exchange, criminal case or communication restriction before the office discusses strategy.

Before contacting the office

Have the county, docket number if known, next date, current order, requested change and involved party names available for conflict-check purposes.
