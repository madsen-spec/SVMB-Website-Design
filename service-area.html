Where the issue is pending

Why the court or agency changes the first step

Two legal issues with the same label can move differently depending on the court, agency, board or office involved. Identifying that place early helps focus preparation on the right papers.

Court of Common Pleas cases

County Court of Common Pleas cases may involve pleadings, scheduling orders, conferences, hearings, and trial listings. County-level procedure can affect what comes next.

Magisterial district court cases

Many criminal, traffic, summary, landlord-tenant, and smaller civil cases begin or proceed at the magisterial district court level. Identify the specific magisterial district court or judge listed on the paperwork when calling.

Agency and board issues

Unemployment compensation referee hearings, PHRC or EEOC paperwork, zoning hearing boards, municipal offices, and other agencies each operate on their own schedules and notices. Save every notice, envelope, and dashboard message.

Pre-suit and negotiation

Some disputes should be evaluated before a complaint is filed. Demand letters, insurance correspondence, contract notice provisions, lease language, property records, and agency letters can shape the next step.

Local context

Say where the issue is pending.

When calling or emailing, use the exact county and court, agency or office shown on the paperwork. Examples include a magisterial district court, Court of Common Pleas, Domestic Relations office, unemployment referee hearing, zoning hearing board, municipal office, PennDOT notice, insurance claim, or civil docket.

If nothing has been filed yet, explain where the event happened, where the property is located, where the employer is located, or where the other party lives or does business. Place often affects what can be done first.

Have a court or agency notice?

Use the exact court or agency name, date and time listed on the notice when calling or emailing the office. Keep confidential details out of email until the office confirms it can speak with you. Contacting the firm does not create an attorney-client relationship.
