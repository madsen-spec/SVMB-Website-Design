Family-member custody

Not every close family relationship gives the same legal footing.

Grandparent custody and visitation questions require careful review before filing. The first issue may be whether the grandparent has legal standing to ask for custody, partial custody or visitation at all.

Useful facts can include the child's living history, parents' relationship status, any prior court orders, parental availability, safety concerns, school stability, caregiving history and the child's routine.

SVMB Law helps Lehigh Valley families sort grandparent custody questions, review existing orders, identify records and decide what court step, if any, should be considered.

Start with the child's history.

Dates, living arrangements, school enrollment, medical care and caregiving records often matter more than a general description of family conflict.

Useful first-call details

  • Relationship to the child
  • Where the child has lived
  • Existing custody orders
  • Current safety or school concerns

Court preparation

The first question may be whether a petition can be filed.

Before discussing a requested schedule, gather facts about standing: the family relationship, the parents' status, existing orders, whether the child lived with the grandparent and whether there are safety or disruption concerns.

If court papers already exist, bring the complete packet. If nothing has been filed, bring the current schedule, child-related records and a short timeline of caregiving and family events.

Existing orders

Bring every custody, PFA, dependency, guardianship or other family-court order involving the child.

Caregiving history

Prepare dates showing when the child lived with or was cared for by the grandparent or other family member.

Parent information

Identify each parent's location, availability, involvement, current schedule and any related court case.

School and medical records

Gather school enrollment, attendance, medical care, therapy, childcare and activity records when relevant.

Safety concerns

Bring police reports, PFA papers, medical records, messages, witness names or agency papers if safety is part of the issue.

Before contacting the office

Have the county, current order, child location, parent names, next date and relationship facts ready for conflict-check purposes.
