Finding the right fit

A first call should point to the next step.

When someone contacts SVMB Law, the first question is not simply which lawyer should handle it. The office needs enough basic information to understand the problem, check conflicts and see whether the firm can help.

A DUI case may involve court papers and PennDOT mail. A custody question may turn on an existing order or conference notice. An employment or injury issue may depend on notices, records or insurance requests. A civil dispute may begin with contracts, property records or court papers.

A brief call with the case type, county, next date and key papers usually helps more at the start than a long background story. Clients may ask for a specific attorney, but the office still needs to check the case type, county, next date, conflicts and attorney availability before confirming who can look at it.

Helpful details to have ready

  • Type of issue.
  • County and the specific court, agency, board, or magisterial district.
  • Next date shown on a notice, citation, order, agency letter or filing.
  • Names of involved parties for conflict-check purposes.
  • Papers already received, including orders, notices, citations, agency papers, contracts, leases or correspondence.

Choosing counsel

Attorney fit depends on the issue, timing and availability.

Clients are welcome to ask for a specific attorney. The office still has to check the case type, county, timing, conflicts and attorney availability before confirming who can look at it.

The same legal category can require different preparation depending on the court, judge, agency, prior history, available proof or wording in the papers. A short first call helps identify which documents need attention first.

Representation begins only after the firm completes a conflict check and confirms engagement.

Before asking for a specific attorney

It is helpful to have these details ready:

  • the county where the issue is pending;
  • the court, agency, board, or office listed on any notice;
  • the next hearing, conference, filing, appeal, or response date;
  • the names of involved parties for conflict-check purposes;
  • the type of document received, such as a citation, complaint, petition, order, letter, agency notice, contract, lease, or insurance request;
  • whether prior counsel has been involved and, if so, the status of that representation.

A short, focused call is usually more useful than a long unsorted history. Keep confidential, privileged or emergency information out of email until the office confirms that it can discuss the issue with you.
