Defense work

Early decisions can shape the case.

Criminal cases in Pennsylvania can begin with an investigation, arrest, citation or complaint. Early papers may set bail conditions, a preliminary hearing, an arraignment or a later court schedule before formal discovery is complete.

An early conversation should identify the charges, court, next date, bail status, papers received and any missing information. Before speaking with police, waiving a hearing, entering a plea or missing a court date, it is important to understand what is being alleged and what choices may be available.

Local court preparation

Start with the charge, the court date and the paperwork.

For an Easton-area criminal case, start with the arresting agency, magisterial district judge, next hearing date, bail status, charges, discovery received and any immediate work, school, license or family concerns. Organized records help an attorney spot factual disputes, missing information and timing problems.

If the case is already in the Northampton County Court of Common Pleas, bring the docket number, scheduling orders, plea paperwork, trial notices, sentencing papers and probation information. If the case is still before a magisterial district judge, bring the complaint, summons, affidavit if available, bail paperwork and preliminary hearing notice.

If there is a warrant, detainer or missed court date, do not assume the issue will resolve itself. The first question is procedural: which court issued the warrant, why it was issued, whether bail was forfeited or revoked, and what paperwork explains the next step.

Preliminary hearings

The preliminary hearing is often the first major court event in a Pennsylvania criminal case. Preparation may include reviewing the complaint, identifying witness issues, preserving defenses, evaluating cross-examination, considering bail concerns and deciding whether contesting, waiving or negotiating fits the facts and client goals.

Warrants, bail and detainers

When a warrant, bail issue, probation detainer or missed hearing is involved, the first question is which court issued it, what triggered it and what step is needed to get back before the right judge. Bring the warrant notice, bail paperwork, probation documents and any scheduling notices.

Negotiation and trial preparation

Resolution may involve diversion, reduced charges, plea negotiations, trial or sentencing advocacy. The right path depends on the record, evidence, client goals and case risk.

Charge-specific records

Drug, theft, assault, firearm, sex-offense, juvenile, PFA-contempt, federal and grand-jury questions may require different records. Bring the complaint, police papers, lab or property records, protection orders, witness information and any government contact letter if those documents exist.

Collateral consequences

Criminal allegations can affect employment, licenses, education, immigration, custody, housing, military status and firearm rights. Those concerns should be raised early so they are considered before bail, plea, hearing and sentencing decisions are made.

Easton criminal defense FAQ

Questions people often ask before calling.

Should I talk to police if they say they only want my side?

Call an attorney before giving a statement. A conversation that seems informal may still affect an investigation, charging decision, bail issue or later testimony. If police are asking for a statement, identify the agency, officer, alleged incident and whether charges have already been filed.

What should I bring for a preliminary hearing review?

Bring the complaint, affidavit if available, summons, bail paperwork, hearing notice, witness information, messages, photos, video and anything showing your location, communications or the timeline of the alleged incident. If you received paperwork by mail, bring the envelope too.

Can a criminal case create work or licensing issues?

Yes. Employment, professional licensing, school, firearm, immigration, housing and family-law consequences can matter before a final disposition. Raise those concerns during the first call so they are not treated as an afterthought.

What if the charge involves drugs, property, firearms or a sex-offense allegation?

Call with the exact charge names and the papers you received. Those cases may involve lab records, alleged property loss, protection-order history, search issues, electronic records, license concerns or other facts that should be checked before decisions are made.

What if the hearing is very soon?

Call the office and identify the court, date, time, judge or magisterial district judge, docket number and charges listed on the paperwork. Do not assume a continuance will be granted. Bring or send the court notice and charging documents as soon as possible.
